Who is it for?

When an adult ADHD assessment may be worth considering

Adults seek an ADHD assessment for many different reasons. Some have experienced long-standing difficulties with attention, organisation, restlessness, impulsivity or completing tasks. Others begin to question ADHD after changes at work, university, parenthood or other periods when demands increase.

An assessment is not simply a symptom checklist. ADHD is a developmental condition, so a clinician needs to consider how your experiences have developed over time, how they affect different areas of life and whether another explanation may better account for some or all of the difficulties.

If your symptoms are affecting your work, education, relationships or everyday functioning, an assessment can help clarify whether ADHD is an appropriate diagnosis and what options may be worth discussing afterwards.

Common reasons people seek assessment

Difficulty sustaining attention or finishing tasks.

Organisation, planning or time-management problems.

Restlessness, impulsive decisions or feeling constantly “on the go”.

Long-standing patterns that have affected education, work, relationships or daily life.

Important: these experiences can have many causes and do not by themselves confirm ADHD.

How it works

What happens during an adult ADHD assessment?

The aim is to gather enough information to understand both current difficulties and the longer-term picture.

1

Pre-assessment information

We gather essential details and may ask you to complete screening questionnaires or provide relevant supporting information before the appointment.

2

Structured clinical assessment

A clinician explores symptoms, childhood and developmental history, education or work, relationships, daily functioning, health and other relevant factors.

3

Outcome and report

You receive clear feedback about the clinical outcome, written documentation and guidance on appropriate next steps.

Diagnosis is not guaranteed. The purpose of the assessment is to reach the most appropriate clinical conclusion from the information available — whether or not that conclusion is ADHD.
Beyond diagnosis

Understanding your treatment options.

Our planned adult service brings assessment and follow-up care together. What happens next depends on your needs, preferences and clinical suitability.

Medication initiation

A separate consultation to discuss whether medication is appropriate, review the required health information and agree a treatment plan.

Titration

Planned follow-up while treatment is adjusted, with an explanation of the review process and how to raise concerns between appointments.

Ongoing care

Clear arrangements for continuing reviews, monitoring and prescribing, including discussion of shared care where appropriate.

Medication is a choice, not an automatic next step. Practical adjustments and non-medication approaches may also be relevant. Read the NHS overview of adult ADHD support.

A broader view

Understanding the whole picture

A good ADHD assessment considers context and alternatives as well as symptoms.

Current experiences

Attention, activity levels, impulsivity, organisation, emotional regulation and the practical impact of difficulties on day-to-day life.

Developmental history

Whether relevant patterns were present earlier in life, including school experiences, family observations and any historical information that is available.

The wider clinical picture

Physical health, mental health, sleep, substance use, other neurodevelopmental differences and alternative explanations that may need consideration.

Preparing

What should I prepare before an ADHD assessment?

You do not need to arrive with a perfectly organised life history. It can help to think about specific examples of how your difficulties affect home, work, education, relationships and everyday responsibilities.

If you have them, old school reports, previous clinical letters or information from somebody who knew you when you were younger may provide useful context. Not everyone has access to childhood records or a suitable informant, so this should be discussed rather than treated as an automatic barrier.

It is also helpful to have an up-to-date list of medicines, relevant health conditions and previous mental health or neurodevelopmental assessments.

FAQ

Adult ADHD assessment FAQs

Clear answers to common questions before you decide whether to register interest.

When will adult ADHD assessments launch?+

Adult ADHD assessments are currently coming soon. You can register your interest and we will contact you when appointments open.

How long is the adult ADHD assessment?+

We will confirm the appointment length and what is included before you book. The assessment may involve questionnaires, supporting information and additional discussion or follow-up where needed; it is not limited to a single checklist.

What does an adult ADHD assessment look at?+

The assessment is designed to consider current symptoms, developmental history, day-to-day functioning, education or work, relationships, physical and mental health history, and other factors that may help explain your experiences.

Do I need information from someone who knew me as a child?+

Supporting information can be useful because ADHD is a developmental condition. Where it is available, a clinician may ask for collateral information such as school reports or input from someone who knows you well. If this is not available, the clinician can discuss what other evidence may be helpful.

Will an assessment definitely result in an ADHD diagnosis?+

No. A diagnosis is never guaranteed. The outcome depends on the full clinical assessment and the information available.

Can an ADHD assessment be completed online?+

Our planned private adult ADHD pathway is designed to be delivered remotely for suitable people across the UK. Suitability is considered during triage, and an alternative pathway may be recommended where remote assessment is not appropriate.

When will I receive my report?+

Your report will be issued once the clinician has reviewed the required information and completed the relevant checks. We will confirm the expected turnaround before you book and explain any additional steps that may affect the timing.

Will My Neuro Health offer medication initiation and titration?+

Yes. Medication initiation and titration form part of our planned adult ADHD service, where clinically appropriate. The service is coming soon; treatment availability, eligibility and fees will be confirmed before bookings open. An assessment does not guarantee a diagnosis or a prescription.
