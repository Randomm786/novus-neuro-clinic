Adult ADHD assessment

A thorough assessment. A clear outcome.

Our assessment pathway is being designed to explore symptoms, developmental history, functional impact and relevant alternative explanations in a structured, respectful way.

What assessment involves

More than a symptom checklist.

An ADHD assessment should build a coherent clinical picture. Information may include current experiences, childhood history, impact across settings and relevant physical or mental health considerations.

✓
Developmental history
Understanding patterns over time.
✓
Functional impact
How difficulties affect work, relationships and daily life.
✓
Differential considerations
Considering whether something else may better explain symptoms.
The goal is understanding, not simply a label.A diagnosis is only appropriate when the clinical evidence supports it.

When the picture is complex

Not every presentation fits neatly into one box.

Neurodevelopmental symptoms can overlap with mental health conditions, sleep problems, other neurodevelopmental conditions and wider clinical factors. The planned Novus model includes clear escalation routes so that cases involving diagnostic uncertainty or complexity can receive appropriate specialist review rather than being forced through a standard pathway.

Diagnostic uncertainty

Additional information or specialist review may be required before a conclusion is reached.

Overlapping needs

Assessment should consider relevant alternative and coexisting explanations.

Patient voice

Your lived experience and functional impact remain central to the assessment.

Understanding

The outcome should help you understand your needs and appropriate next steps, whether or not ADHD is diagnosed.
