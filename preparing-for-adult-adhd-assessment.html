How to prepare for an adult ADHD assessment

You do not need to create a perfect dossier. A few practical preparations can make it easier to give the clinician a clear picture.

Write down real examples

Think about situations where attention, organisation, time management, restlessness or impulsivity have created difficulties. Examples from work, study, home, finances, relationships and everyday responsibilities are often more useful than trying to remember a list of symptoms.

Think back to childhood

Consider what school was like, how you managed homework, deadlines, behaviour, friendships and organisation. If you have old school reports or another reliable source of childhood information, keep them available.

Review your health history

Have an up-to-date list of medicines and relevant physical or mental health conditions. Previous assessment reports or clinical letters may also be useful where relevant.

Ask someone who knows you well

A clinician may find supporting information from a partner, parent, sibling or close friend useful. The person does not need to diagnose you; they are simply describing what they have observed.

Bring your questions

Write down anything you want clarified about the assessment, diagnosis, report or next steps. It is easy to forget questions during a long appointment.

General information only: This article is educational and is not a diagnosis or individual medical advice.
