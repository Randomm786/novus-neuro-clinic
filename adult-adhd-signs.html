Adult ADHD signs: when might an assessment be worth considering?

ADHD can look different from person to person. The important question is not whether you recognise one symptom, but whether a persistent pattern is affecting everyday life.

Attention and organisation

Some adults describe being easily distracted, losing track of tasks, forgetting appointments, misplacing items or finding planning and prioritising unusually difficult.

Restlessness and impulsivity

Hyperactivity in adults may feel more like internal restlessness than obvious physical overactivity. Some people also describe interrupting, acting quickly without enough time to think or finding waiting particularly difficult.

Impact matters

Many people occasionally struggle with concentration or organisation. In an ADHD assessment, the clinician considers whether a persistent pattern causes meaningful difficulty in areas such as work, education, relationships or everyday responsibilities.

ADHD is developmental

The assessment also looks at whether relevant difficulties were present earlier in life. Adult circumstances can change how symptoms appear, but ADHD does not suddenly begin for the first time in adulthood.

Other explanations need consideration

Sleep problems, stress, anxiety, depression, substance use, other neurodevelopmental conditions and physical health problems can overlap with ADHD-like experiences. This is one reason self-screening cannot replace a clinical assessment.
