What happens during an adult ADHD assessment?

A practical guide to the information a clinician may explore and why an ADHD assessment looks at more than a symptom checklist.

Before the appointment

You may be asked to complete questionnaires and provide background information before the clinical appointment. This helps the clinician understand the reason for referral and identify areas that need more detailed discussion.

The clinical conversation

The clinician will usually explore attention, activity levels, impulsivity, organisation and how difficulties affect everyday life. The discussion may cover work or education, relationships, daily responsibilities, physical and mental health, sleep and other relevant factors.

Childhood and developmental history

Because ADHD is a developmental condition, the assessment considers whether relevant patterns were present earlier in life. School reports, previous records or information from somebody who knew you as a child can sometimes be helpful, although not everyone will have access to these.

Considering other explanations

Symptoms such as poor concentration, restlessness or difficulty completing tasks are not unique to ADHD. A thorough assessment considers whether another condition, circumstance or combination of factors could better explain the difficulties.

The outcome

At the end of the process, the clinician brings the information together and explains the clinical conclusion. A diagnosis is not guaranteed. If ADHD is diagnosed, the discussion should also cover what the diagnosis means and appropriate next steps.

General information only: This article is educational and is not a diagnosis or individual medical advice.
