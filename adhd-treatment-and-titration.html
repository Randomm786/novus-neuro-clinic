Your choices matter

More than one way forward.

An ADHD diagnosis does not mean you have to take medication. Support may include practical changes at home, work or university, and psychological approaches where appropriate. Your preferences and the impact on your daily life should shape the discussion.

Our planned consultation will give you space to ask questions about the options, their possible benefits and downsides, and what each route would involve. We will explain which support we provide and when another service may be more suitable.

Medication initiation

First, understand whether treatment is suitable.

Initiation means the decision to start medication and the arrangements for doing so. It is a separate clinical step, not an automatic result of assessment.

Discuss the decision

We plan a consultation about your goals, questions and preferences, including the options available and what monitoring would involve. A prescription is never guaranteed.

Review health information

Before medication, checks include medical history, current medicines, height, weight, pulse, blood pressure and cardiovascular health. Your clinician may need further information or assessment.

Agree the next steps

Where treatment is appropriate, we will explain the proposed review arrangements, costs and how to contact the clinical team with concerns before you decide to proceed.

Physical checks are part of medication suitability assessment. NICE NG87: baseline assessment.

Understanding titration

Adjusting treatment, with regular review.

Titration means adjusting a medication dose while checking how you respond. The aim is useful benefit with acceptable side effects, rather than reaching a particular dose as quickly as possible.

Reviews consider symptoms, everyday functioning, side effects and relevant physical-health observations. The clinician may adjust the plan if treatment is not helping or is not well tolerated. There is no guaranteed number of appointments or fixed time in which treatment will become stable.

For our planned service, we will explain the contact and monitoring schedule before you start. Any package will state what is included and how additional reviews are arranged and charged.

Royal College of Psychiatrists: adult ADHD treatment guidance (PDF).

What to bring to a review

Notes about what has changed in everyday life, questions you want to ask and any concerns since your last appointment.

The monitoring information your prescriber has asked for, with an up-to-date list of medicines or supplements.

We will confirm how to share this information securely. Do not send detailed medical records through the public enquiry form.

After titration

A clear plan for continuing care.

Before you start treatment, you should know who will be responsible for reviews and prescribing afterwards.

Monitoring and reviews

Stable treatment still needs review. NICE recommends checking adult weight, blood pressure and pulse at least every six months, with pulse and blood pressure also checked around dose changes. Medication should be reviewed by an appropriately trained professional at least annually; more frequent checks may be needed.

Shared care with your GP

Shared care can allow a GP to take over agreed prescribing responsibilities alongside specialist care. It requires agreement and must not be assumed. A private assessment does not guarantee an NHS prescription.

When shared care is unavailable

We will explain the proposed private follow-up and prescribing options, their costs and any limits before treatment starts. Where our pathway is unsuitable, we will discuss appropriate next steps rather than promise a transfer that has not been agreed.

Your questions

Treatment and titration FAQs

Answers to common questions about the service we are preparing.

Can I book medication initiation or titration now?+

Not yet. The adult service is in preparation. You can register for updates now; we will confirm treatment availability, eligibility, clinician details and fees before appointments open.

Will I definitely be offered medication?+

No. Our planned service includes an individual treatment-suitability discussion. The outcome depends on your needs, preferences and the clinical information available. Assessment or consultation does not guarantee a prescription.

Can I enquire if I already have an ADHD diagnosis?+

Yes, you can register an enquiry. We have not yet confirmed our policy for accepting assessments completed elsewhere. A previous diagnosis does not guarantee acceptance, and we may need further records or reassessment before deciding whether our service is appropriate.

Who will prescribe and monitor treatment?+

We plan for treatment to be provided by appropriately qualified prescribing professionals with ADHD expertise. Clinician profiles, registration details and responsibilities will be published before bookings open. We will identify who is responsible for each stage of your care.

Will every part of treatment be online?+

We are preparing a remote service for suitable adults. Some people will need physical checks, further investigations or specialist input outside a video consultation. We will explain the requirements and arrangements before treatment starts.

How long will titration take?+

We will explain the proposed review schedule before you start. Clinical progress, not a fixed package deadline, determines the next steps. Our pricing information will explain additional reviews when needed.

What if my GP does not agree to shared care?+

We will discuss the available ongoing private-care options, fees and any limits before treatment begins. We will not describe NHS prescribing as a guaranteed outcome or treat an unconfirmed shared-care request as an agreed transfer.

Is this treatment pathway for children too?+

No. This page describes our planned adult service. Children need age-appropriate assessment and treatment arrangements, which will be explained separately before the child pathway opens.
